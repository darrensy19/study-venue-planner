### Findings

#### IMP-006-R1-F01 — Medium — truncated endpoint clock values are accepted and rewritten

- **Status at issuance**: `open`
- **Evidence**: `scraper/hours.py:145-150` validates a truncated open only by date and then forces its minute to zero; `scraper/hours.py:163-171` validates a truncated close only by date and then forces it to 1440. The settled contract requires `open.truncated` only at the window's first date **and 00:00**, and a truncated close only at the final-date boundary (`CLAUDE.md:101`; `HANDOFF.md` acceptance criteria). First-hand reproduction with `tests/python/fixtures/open_and_close_truncated.json` changed the truncated open to 12:00 and parsed it as `{open: 0, close: 2850}`; changing the truncated close to 12:59 parsed it as `{open: 390, close: 1440, continues_beyond_window: true}`. Neither `tests/python/test_hours.py:122-138` nor another test exercises a wrong clock value at an otherwise valid boundary date.
- **Impact**: Malformed source data silently fabricates coverage to a window edge, instead of failing loudly as required. That can overstate both known opening time and continuity beyond the window.
- **Recommended correction**: Reject a truncated open unless its reported time is exactly 00:00, and reject a truncated close unless it is the documented final-date boundary representation (23:59 before normalisation); add focused negative tests for both date-correct, time-incorrect shapes.
